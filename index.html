Making AI that decides under pressure fair and testable

Sraboni Bhuiyan

Abstract

I study how language-model systems behave when the stakes are high and the data is uneven. My master's thesis builds a fairness-aware retrieval-augmented triage assistant for emergency care, and I approach it with the habits of a software tester: define the failure, measure it, then try to break the fix.

I came to research from computer science and engineering, and I currently test secure communication software in industry. I am looking for a group where fairness, evaluation and clinical decision support are treated as one problem.

01Research interests

Three questions I keep returning to.

  1. RQ1

    Where does retrieval introduce bias?

    When a model answers from retrieved documents, the retriever decides what evidence it sees. I want to measure whether that evidence differs across patient groups, and how much of the final answer's disparity it explains.

  2. RQ2

    How should a triage assistant be evaluated?

    Accuracy alone hides who the system fails. I am interested in evaluation protocols that report performance per subgroup, under rare and ambiguous cases, with clinicians in the loop.

  3. RQ3

    Can testing practice transfer to AI systems?

    Regression suites, edge-case catalogues and traceability are routine in software QA. I want to adapt them to probabilistic systems so that fairness claims can be re-checked after every change.

02Master's thesis

A fairness-aware RAG assistant for emergency-care triage

The thesis designs and evaluates a retrieval-augmented assistant that supports triage decisions in emergency care, with fairness built into how evidence is retrieved and how answers are checked, rather than added afterwards.
